How It Went Down

The first round played out about as predictably as a room full of 40-year friends who've done this too many times can manage — RB after RB (Gibbs, Bijan, Jonathan Taylor) before Ja'Marr Chase snuck in at 1.03 to prove receivers still exist. By pick 12 the board had gone seven RBs deep in round one alone, and Waffle House doubled right back down, taking Ashton Jeanty one pick after Saquon Barkley to open round two — a gutsy 'ignore the position run, start your own' move. Quarterback was this draft's designated hostage. Josh Allen went at 2.06 and then the position just... stopped existing for nineteen picks. Nobody blinked. The QB1 board didn't refill until round four, and even then it trickled out one at a time all the way to pick 122, where Pukachu Chews You finally took Bo Nix in round 11 because apparently someone had to. Relative to how sluggishly this room approached passers, Broke Dak Mountain's Josh Allen at 2.06 looks like the earliest sniper shot of the entire draft — nobody else even considered the position for another three rounds. Wide receiver, on the other hand, never really stopped. After the RB-heavy round one, this room strung together WR run after WR run — three-to-four player windows at picks 8-11, 9-12, 33-36, 49-51, and again late in round 9 into round 10. Six different teams ended up with 6+ receivers by the end of the draft, which tells you this group collectively decided the position was too deep to worry about scarcity — they just kept circling back. Tight end was the position everyone loves to say they'll take early and then doesn't. Brock Bowers went at pick 26 (3.02) as the first TE off the board, which is actually aggressive relative to what followed — Trey McBride went four picks later, and then the position went ice cold, taking eleven rounds to fully empty out, ending with A.J. Barner and Dallas Goedert-types in round 15. And defenses did exactly what defenses do in a 12-team snake with 15 rounds: nothing happened until pick 92, and by the time the last one went off the board (Steelers, pick 170), half the room had probably forgotten what a streaming plan was.
